Filling and Signing PDF Forms Online

Printing a form, signing it by hand, scanning it and emailing it back is a ritual almost nobody enjoys. You can often skip all of it with a PDF that is already on your screen. The catch is that "filling a form" and "signing a form" are two different jobs, and PDFLeo has a separate tool for each, with different strengths and limits.

This guide explains which tool to use when, how to fill fields reliably, what the typed signature feature really is, and what to check before sending.

Start by finding out what kind of PDF you have

PDF forms come in two flavours, and the first thing to learn is which one you are holding.

  • Interactive forms contain real fields, often called AcroForm fields. When you click into a box in a PDF reader, a cursor appears and you can type. Tax forms, applications and many government documents are built this way.
  • Flat forms look like forms but are just a picture of one. Clicking does nothing. These come from scans, or from documents that were printed to PDF with the fields removed.

To test, open the PDF in your browser or reader and click a blank box. If it highlights fields or lets you type, it is interactive. Fields are also often tinted in colour when you open the file.

Filling interactive forms with Fill PDF Forms

The Fill PDF Forms tool reads the fields in the PDF you choose and builds a simple list of inputs. You type your answers, run the tool and download a filled copy. It supports:

  • Text fields, for names, dates, addresses and numbers.
  • Checkboxes, ticked or unticked.
  • Choice fields, such as dropdown lists, where you pick from the listed options.

Other kinds of field, for example buttons or signature fields, are shown in the list marked as unsupported, and the tool leaves them alone. It cannot create new fields. If a PDF has no interactive fields, the tool tells you so rather than guessing.

Step by step

  1. Gather what you need first: names as they appear on your ID, reference numbers, dates in the format the form expects.
  2. Open Fill PDF Forms and choose the PDF.
  3. Wait for the field list to appear. Field names come from the PDF itself, so they can look technical, such as "Text1" or "applicant_surname". If names are unclear, open the PDF in a reader beside the tool to see which box is which.
  4. Fill in the fields, tick the checkboxes and choose options.
  5. Run the tool and download the result.
  6. Open the new file and check every field.

Why checking matters

The tool writes your values into the fields and refreshes how they look. Some forms use custom formatting, such as automatic date masks or calculated totals produced by scripts inside the PDF. Those scripts may not run the way they do in a full reader. So a total you expected to see calculated might still be empty. Look at the filled file in a normal reader and correct anything unusual.

Also note that the fields in the saved copy stay editable. That is often useful, because you can fix a typo in a reader later, but it also means the recipient can change values. If you need the answers locked, ask the recipient what format they accept, or produce a flattened print-to-PDF version in your system's print dialog.

What if the form is flat?

If there are no fields, Fill PDF Forms cannot help. Two practical options exist:

  • Use Edit PDF to place text at chosen positions. You choose the page, the X and Y position in points measured from the bottom-left, and the text. It takes some trial and error to line text up with the boxes, but it works on any PDF. Make a first try, look at the result, adjust the numbers and run again.
  • Print, write and scan, as a last resort, then convert the scan with Scan to PDF or JPG to PDF.

Adding a signature with Sign PDF

The Sign PDF tool does something specific, and it is worth being clear about it. You type your name, choose which pages receive it, set the position in points from the left and from the bottom, and pick a font size. The tool then writes that text onto the pages. It is a typed signature mark.

What it is not:

  • It is not a handwritten signature, and it does not capture your pen strokes.
  • It is not a digital signature backed by a certificate. PDFLeo states this plainly in the tool, and nothing in the file can prove who added the text or that the document was not changed afterwards.

Whether a typed name is acceptable depends on who asked for the signature. Many everyday situations, such as a casual agreement, an internal approval or a permission slip, accept it. Contracts, official filings, and anything where the receiving party needs a particular kind of electronic or handwritten signature may not. When in doubt, ask the recipient which type they need before you spend time on it.

If you want your actual handwriting, sign on paper, photograph or scan the signature on white paper, crop it and save it as an image. Then use Edit PDF, which can place an image such as a logo or signature picture on chosen pages. Keep the picture's background clean, and keep the image file private, since anyone who gets it can reuse your signature.

Placing the signature

Coordinates can be confusing the first time. Both X and Y are in points, where 72 points equal one inch. X counts from the left edge. Y counts from the bottom edge, not the top. On a US Letter page, which is 612 by 792 points, a signature at X 72 and Y 100 sits one inch from the left and a little under 1.5 inches up from the bottom. Leaving the page range empty applies the mark to every page, so type specific pages such as 3 or 1-3,5 if only one page needs it.

A safe order of work

  1. Fill the fields first.
  2. Check the filled version in a reader.
  3. Add the signature last, on the page where it is required.
  4. Name the final file clearly, for example application-john-smith-signed.pdf.
  5. If the file contains private data and is going by email, consider password protection and its limits. Note that Protect PDF turns pages into images, so form fields will not remain usable afterwards. Do it last.

Before you send it

  • Every required field is filled and the right box is ticked.
  • Dates and numbers are in the format requested.
  • The signature sits on the signature line and does not overlap printed text.
  • You kept a copy of the original blank form.
  • The recipient accepts this kind of file and signature.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the tool say there are no fillable fields?

The PDF is probably flat, meaning it is a picture of a form. Use Edit PDF to place text, or request an interactive version from the sender.

Are my form answers uploaded?

The tools run in your browser, so the file and what you type are not sent to a PDFLeo server. Library files are downloaded when first needed.

Can I save the form and finish later?

You can download the partly filled PDF and reopen it in Fill PDF Forms or any reader later, because the fields stay editable.

Is a typed signature legally binding?

That depends on your country, the document and the receiving party, and we cannot give legal advice. Ask the recipient what they accept.
